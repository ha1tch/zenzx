# Acknowledgements

ZenZX, and the wider family of Spectrum tools it sits among, owes rather a lot to
the friends who have kept me company along the way. For the better part of three
years the people below have been generous with encouragement, with a well-aimed
tip at the right moment, and with the sort of good, unhurried conversation that
quietly keeps a project going. They have followed along from the sidelines; they
have made the journey better, and I'm grateful to them.

- **Thomas Sturm** — programmer, photographer and writer; his blog is at
  [sturm.to](https://sturm.to), and he is on the Fediverse at
  [@tsturm@famichiki.jp](https://famichiki.jp/@tsturm). To make matters worse, as
  well as being an accomplished baker he has found the time to write an emulator of
  his own, [BitPrism](https://gitlab.com/thomassturm/bitprism), which brings the
  power of Metal and 3D to the ZX Spectrum on the Mac, and a marvellous shoot 'em
  up that is, as yet, unreleased. I mention it here purely so that he feels the
  gentle, unrelenting weight of my expectation. Ship it, Thomas.
- **Martian Girl / Marta** — author of the SpecIde emulator
  ([codeberg.org/MartianGirl/SpecIde](https://codeberg.org/MartianGirl/SpecIde)).
  Her help has been fundamental and unfailing throughout, and SpecIde itself is a
  first-rate reference — any emulator developer stands to learn a good deal from
  reading it. It is rare to find anyone as dedicated, serious, studious and
  generous, and she has been kind enough to share all of that with us.
- **Steve Wetherill** — something of a legend in games, in almost any genre and at
  any scale, from boutique studios to titles that reach millions: if you have
  played video games in the last three or four decades, you or someone you know
  has played one of his. On the retro side he wrote *Nodes of Yesod* and a good many Spectrum,
  Atari ST and Amiga titles, after cutting his teeth at Software Projects porting
  *Manic Miner* and *Jet Set Willy* to the Amstrad CPC. He writes a [blog](https://blog.stevewetherill.com)
  and is serialising his memoir on [Substack](https://stevewetherill.substack.com),
  the story of a journey from 8-bit bedroom coder to global franchises — a terrific
  read, sharp, very funny, and free into the bargain.
- **Jim Blimey** — programmer, indie and retro game developer and video blogger, with
  many Spectrum projects to his credit at [www.jimblimey.com](https://www.jimblimey.com).
  That description undersells him: he is a reliable source of support and good
  laughs, and he tested the code when few others dared, always ready to lend a
  helping hand and always with a good joke to go with it. He runs a whole host of
  Spectrums; his favourite is his 128K "toastrack", though it is his Omni that we
  see more often on his Twitch live casts, since this little modern wonder is more
  convenient for streaming. His help cannot be overestimated.
- **Rodolfo Guerra** — he has helped with the internationalisation of ZenZX and with
  testing it, and his knowledge of the Spectrum is vast, above all of the Brazilian
  and Uruguayan clones by Microdigital, the TK90X and the TK95: he is the author of the
  book *Generación TK90X*, the history of the TK90X, built on some forty years of research
  and the testimony of collaborators from Brazil, Spain and Uruguay. He supplied the package of all the historical Microdigital
  ROMs, the originals, and the five of them that ZenZX ships in custom-roms/Microdigital/ are byte
  for byte his. He also has a [website](https://sites.google.com/view/rodolfoguerra)
  where he publishes his modified ROMs, free for anyone to use. Many thanks, Rodolfo.

Thanks, too, to the wider #retrocomputing and ZX Spectrum lot on
[oldbytes.space](https://oldbytes.space) and across the Fediverse — a good-natured,
endlessly curious crowd, and no small part of why this corner of the hobby is such
pleasant company to keep.

Thank you all.
